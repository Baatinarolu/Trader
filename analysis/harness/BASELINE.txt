# Backtest baselines — what changed, and why the older numbers must not be quoted

`node analysis/harness/backtest.js --seeds 60 --bars 1500 --window 300`
Engine: `src/bots/smc.js analyse()` + `src/bots/setup.js buildSetups()`, unmodified call paths.

⚠ **Synthetic data.** These figures measure the internal consistency of the entry / stop / target
logic. They are not real-market edge, and the cost model is ~1.57× optimistic (M27).

---

## ⚠ SUPERSEDED — the wall-clock era

Everything recorded before this pass was run with the killzone clock read from `new Date()`, so
**the numbers depended on the hour the command happened to be typed at.**

| | n | win | exp | PF | A+ | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| old "baseline" | 349 | 20.3 % | −0.3201R | 0.46 | 52 | 236 | 36 | 25 |

The headline finding built on that table — *"the grade ladder is inverted; A+ is the worst cut;
the most reproducible finding the harness has produced"* — **was an artefact.** With the clock
walking the bars, A+ became the *best* cut (−0.1848R vs A −0.1886R). The claim was not
reproducible at all. Tracked as **M118**.

**Proof that the clock was the cause** (40 seeds, 236 trades, identical overall expectancy in
every run — only the letters move):

| clock | sessions.quality | A+ | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 08:30Z (London) | 0.85 | 40 · −0.4331 | 157 · −0.2122 | 21 · −0.6140 | 18 · −0.3034 |
| 13:30Z (NY AM) | 1.00 | 40 · −0.4331 | 162 · −0.2365 | 16 · −0.4934 | 18 · −0.3034 |
| 22:30Z (Sydney) | 0.30 | **none** | 40 · −0.4331 | 162 · −0.2365 | 34 · −0.3928 |
| walking clock | varies | 12 · −0.1848 | 97 · −0.1886 | 104 · −0.4147 | 23 · −0.2328 |

At 22:30Z the entire distribution shifts one letter down and A+ ceases to exist. Cause:
`score = score * (0.82 + 0.18 * sessions.quality)` combined with
`sessionState(date = new Date())` called with no argument. Identical candles averaged 30.57
at 22:30Z vs 33.13 at 12:30Z.

## Baseline 2 — CURRENT. Group A vetoes applied (M7, M8, M35, M96)

```
n= 156  win= 22.4%  exp=-0.2845R  PF=0.54
  A+  n=  22  win= 13.6%  exp=-0.5553R  PF=0.34
  A   n= 123  win= 25.2%  exp=-0.2401R  PF=0.59
  B   n=  11  win=  9.1%  exp=-0.2386R  PF=0.56
  C   n=   0        <- the RR veto eliminated this grade entirely
declared 3.73R · target 24 · stop 95 · expired 14
flat 1%: -$4438  maxDD $4953  sd $111.17   |  sized: -$4307  maxDD $4822  sd $107.57
```

**What the vetoes did.** 193 of 349 trades (55 %) are now refused: sub-2R, outside a killzone,
no sweep, or price still chasing. Derived from the two runs above — not measured directly:

```
kept     n=156  exp=-0.2845R
removed  n=193  exp=-0.3489R   = (349*-0.3201 - 156*-0.2845) / 193
```

The refused trades were **0.0644R worse** than the ones kept. That is the right direction, but
0.0356R of improvement at n=156 is **inside the noise** (~0.1R at this sample size) — **do not
report the vetoes as having improved expectancy.** What is defensible is the selection: they
remove the trades the course says to refuse, and the removed set is worse.

**The M95 decision became nearly free.** Cost of keeping B and C tradeable:

| | A+/A only | B/C only | penalty for keeping B and C |
|---|---|---|---|
| before vetoes | −0.2900R (n=156) | −0.3445R (n=193) | **0.0545R** |
| after vetoes | −0.2879R (n=145) | −0.2386R (n=11) | **0.0035R** |

Reason: low-probability ideas are now refused **on their merits** rather than by letter, so the
letters that survive are close to equivalent. The user's decision to keep B and C signalled no
longer costs a measurable amount of expectancy.

**A+ is again the weakest cut** (−0.5553R at n=22). n=22 is far too small to conclude anything,
and M118 is a standing warning about exactly this class of claim. Recorded, not acted on.
The open items that would actually address it are **M8** (thresholds never derived) and **M70**
(grade not validated against realised outcomes).

## M1 — A/B of the swing definition (`--swingmode local|bos`)

`local` = the code's local-extremum tier (default, unchanged). `bos` = the course's "extreme that
created the break of structure". Opt-in only; the default path is verified identical.

Standalone measurement first (`analysis/probe-m1-swing-definition.js`, 40 series x 300 bars,
through the project's own `marketStructure()`): keeps 46 % of the swings, swing range 3.5x wider,
**trend label changes on 63 % of series**. Material — a strategy change, not a refinement.

Then backtested at four sample sizes:

| seeds | n (local) | exp (local) | A+ (local) | n (bos) | exp (bos) | **A+ (bos)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 57 | −0.2587R | +0.3083 (n=7) | 58 | −0.1627R | **+2.1154 (n=6)** |
| 30 | 81 | −0.1685R | −0.2475 (n=13) | 86 | −0.1796R | **+1.1482 (n=11)** |
| 45 | 123 | −0.2405R | −0.4245 (n=17) | 125 | −0.2425R | **+0.6971 (n=17)** |
| 60 | 156 | −0.2845R | −0.5553 (n=22) | 155 | −0.2717R | **+0.2550 (n=25)** |

Read honestly:

- **Overall expectancy barely moves** (−0.2845 → −0.2717 at 60 seeds). The swing definition is not
  an edge; it re-orders which trades receive the top grade.
- **The A+ sign flips and stays flipped.** With `local`, A+ is the worst cut at every size from 30
  seeds up. With `bos`, A+ is positive at all four sizes, shrinking as n grows.
- At n=25, +0.2550R with sd ≈ 1.3R is roughly one standard error from zero. **That is a hypothesis,
  not evidence of an edge** — and after M118 this project does not get to promote a grade cut on
  one sample size.

It does reframe the retracted M118 finding: "A+ is the worst cut" may have been a real symptom
whose cause was the swing definition, not the clock. Both were present; only the clock was
disproved.

M2 fell out of the same pass: `structure.swing_range` is now emitted, and **price sits inside it
on only 6 of 19 series** — which is the evidence that M22 (major tier defines the range, minor
tier read inside it) is not cosmetic.

## M22 — A/B of the nested swing range (`--nested`)

`analysis/probe-m22-nested-range.js` measured first, through the project's own `marketStructure()`:
**56 % of minor swings (887/1587) sit outside the range the major tier defines**; structure events
halve (17.4 → 8.4 per series); the internal trend label changes on 20 % of series.

The range is also unstable — its width runs **0.18 % to 21 % of price** across the same 40 series,
a 100× spread, because it is just the most recent major high and low. That is the same instability
M2 exposed from the other side.

| seeds | n | exp | **A+** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 64 | −0.1584R | +1.3621 (n=7) |
| 30 | 95 | −0.1358R | +0.2256 (n=14) |
| 45 | 141 | −0.2244R | +0.2049 (n=16) |
| 60 | 176 | −0.2604R | +0.1098 (n=21) |

Against the default (parallel tiers): A+ −0.5553R at n=22.

**Standard errors, using the harness's own per-trade sd of ≈1.11R (from the M58 block):**
SE at n≈21 is ±0.24R. So

- default A+ −0.5553R is **≈2.3 SE below zero — reliably negative**
- nested A+ +0.1098R is **within 0.5 SE of zero — indistinguishable from zero**

The defensible claim is therefore *not* "nesting makes A+ profitable" but **"nesting removes a
reliably bad A+ cut."** Stated that way it survives the noise; stated the other way it would not.

## M1 + M22 together — the better hypothesis

Two independent corrections to the structure *inputs* — the swing definition (M1) and the range the
minor tier is read inside (M22) — **both remove the same A+ anomaly**:

| | A+ at 60 seeds |
|---|---|
| default | −0.5553R (n=22), ≈2.3 SE below zero |
| `--swingmode bos` (M1) | +0.2550R (n=25) |
| `--nested` (M22) | +0.1098R (n=21) |

That points at the grade ladder being **fed bad structure**, rather than the grading being wrong.
It is a better hypothesis than the one M118 retracted — and unlike that one, it is consistent
across four sample sizes and two independent mechanisms. It is still synthetic data, and both
magnitudes are within about one standard error, so it remains a hypothesis.

Both ship **opt-in and off by default**. The default 60-seed backtest is unchanged at
n=156 / −0.2845R, which is the proof they are inert until switched on.

## M32 — measured, and why it is opt-in

`--ob-fvg` requires an imbalance in the leg that created the block (Ep 10: "no imbalance, no fair
value gap"). **43 % of order blocks (137/321) fail it.**

| | n | exp | PF |
|---|---|---|---|
| default | 194 | −0.2968R | 0.52 |
| `--ob-fvg` | **19** | −0.1959R | 0.73 |

An 88 % collapse in signal, at n=19 — far too few to validate anything. Two reasons it stays off:

1. **The user's M16 decision explicitly sanctions "entering on OB mitigation when no FVG exists."**
   Making this the default would contradict a binding decision.
2. **The codebase has no separate supply/demand zone construct** — only `kind: 'OB'` and
   `kind: 'FVG'` — though Ep 10 distinguishes them exactly ("for supply and demand zone it doesn't
   matter whether there's imbalance or not... but for order block... it needs an imbalance"). One
   object serves both roles and is the only point-of-interest supply, so the order-block rule
   collapses the POI pool. **The faithful fix is to split the two concepts, not to flip the flag.**

## M44 — a negative result, recorded as one (`--all-swings`)

Pool starvation was real and is now quantified: **6.4 of 14.0 minor swings per series became pools
— 46 % of swings could ever be seen.** With `allSwings`, pools rise **235 → 356 (+51 %)**.

| | n | exp | A+ |
|---|---|---|---|
| default | 194 | −0.2968R | −0.5248R (n=27) |
| `--all-swings` | 191 | −0.3054R | **−0.7401R (n=25)** |

A 51 % larger pool supply changes almost nothing and slightly hurts. **The rule is faithful to the
course ("behind any high or low that price has not yet traded") and the code now offers it, but the
evidence argues against turning it on.** Recorded rather than shipped as a default.

**The trap worth keeping:** expanding the candidate set *without* raising `maxPools` makes new
pools **displace** old ones. At cap 16 the pool count rose 20 % while **sweep events fell 82 → 76**,
because recently-swept levels the trigger needs were pushed out by stronger distant ones. Sweeps
stop shrinking at cap 24 and saturate by 32 (356 pools at 32 vs 357 at 48), so the cap is raised
only when `allSwings` is on.

**And what the fix deliberately avoids:** the ledger suggested filtering on `!swept` at
construction. That would be wrong — `swept` is patched onto these objects later and `findSweeps()`
reads the same list to find the trigger, so filtering there deletes the events the entry model
depends on.

## M47 — flip zones as a point of interest (`--breakers`). The strongest result measured.

Wiring them in was not enough: they were added and then never won, losing the POI ranking **0 of 6**
series where both were eligible. Instrumenting the scores found the cause.

**The bug.** The strength formula penalises a breached zone by **−0.5**, and `findBreakers` reads
that *already-penalised* strength and then takes a further **−0.2**. The breach is the *precondition*
for a flip zone existing — "a supply flip zone is formed when a demand zone fails" — so it was
punished for the thing that created it. Retested breakers averaged **0.355** strength against
**0.898** for un-breached order blocks. Restoring the pre-breach strength gives **0.855**.

**Second asymmetry.** `fresh` keyed to `bars_ago <= 30` gave **0%** of breakers the +0.35 bonus
against **90%** of order blocks. His three criteria are failed reaction, break of structure, close
beyond the reaction point — none is recency. Re-keyed to the retest.

**Two more found by reading every place the zone is re-described**, both of which would have shipped
silently: `setup.js:276` rebuilds the zone as a NEW object with `kind:'order_block'` hardcoded and
`entry: body_mid` (which `findBreakers` does not copy) — so a flip-zone POI would have been
advertised as an order block **with an undefined entry price**. And `stop_reason`'s two-way ternary
would have called a flip zone an "FVG".

| seeds | default n / exp / win / PF | `--breakers` n / exp / win / PF |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | 66 / −0.3149R / 21.2% / 0.50 | 102 / **−0.1739R** / 27.5% / 0.69 |
| 45 | 156 / −0.2850R / 22.4% / 0.54 | 220 / **−0.1580R** / 27.7% / 0.72 |
| 60 | 190 / −0.2891R / 22.6% / 0.53 | 276 / **−0.1847R** / 27.2% / 0.67 |

A+ improves at all three: +0.3927 → +0.8003R, −0.3650 → **+0.1405R**, −0.4590 → **−0.0811R**.

**Every metric improves at every sample size.** Expectancy is still negative overall, so this is not
a claim of edge — but it is the first change measured here that improves trade count, win rate,
profit factor and expectancy together. Left opt-in pending the user's decision, because it raises
the trade count ~45% by supplying zones to setups the Group A veto would otherwise refuse.

**Method note:** my first measurement reported "0 flip zones selected" after the fix and I nearly
concluded the plumbing was broken. It was not — I was reading `candidate.zone`, which does not
exist; candidates carry `levels` and the kind is at `levels.entry_kind`. A +100 score-boost
diagnostic confirmed the plumbing was fine. The bug was in the probe, not the code.

## The M21 ordering bug

`targetPools()` deliberately pushes the weak structural extreme first — "target weak structure".
`setup.js` then ran `targets.sort((a,b) => a.rr - b.rr)`, silently undoing it, so the weak extreme
only led when it happened to be nearest: **77 of 1174** eligible candidates. After restoring the
priority: **1170 of 1170**.

It went unnoticed because the three-rung ladder could surface the weak extreme in *any* rung and the
probe accepted `targets.some(kind==='weak_structure')`. Collapsing to one target removed the hiding
place.

Two probe defects of my own: the original assertion (`withTarget > 0` over *tradeable* candidates)
conflated "the wiring works" with a sampling coincidence; my first replacement compared against the
**candle close** instead of the **entry price** and reported **841 false violations**.

## The §8.2 re-verification sweep — Baseline 6 is unchanged, and one APPLIED row was half-applied

Run this pass with `analysis/probe-reverify-applied.js`, written because §7 of the handoff
records M21 sitting marked APPLIED while silently dead. The question this sweep asks of every
one of the 36 APPLIED rows is not "is the value computed" but **"is the value CONSUMED, and does
it SURVIVE TO THE OUTPUT"**.

**Result: 35 of the 36 survive. M9 did not.** 55 assertions, 0 failures, identical at three

### Two probe defects of my own, recorded because the ledger's rule is to record them

1. **A false failure on M21.** The first assertion was "trending ⇒ both strong and weak
   labelled", which failed 92 of 1 936 trending candidates. `strongWeak()` has a sanctioned
   *third* branch: when the trend label contradicts the swing geometry it emits
   `strong/weak = null` **with `stale: true`** and a note attributing the contradiction to M50
   (no bias hysteresis) rather than inventing a target on the wrong side of the entry. Measured
   directly: 1 844 labelled + 92 stale + **0 unexplained nulls**, and **0** labelled candidates
   with weak on the wrong side of strong. **The probe was wrong, not the code**, so the check was
   rewritten to the real guarantee and the false finding was NOT recorded. §6: check the input
   before reporting the output.

### Reading it correctly — this is a negative result, and it is not evidence against the rule

The harness prints the exact decomposition, `UNIT × Σ gradeMult × (rangeMult−1) × r`
over the cut trades, and it equals the observed delta to the dollar (−$135, −$206).
The NAIVE reading — aggregate weighted R × mean cut — predicts **+$27** and **+$102**,
the **opposite sign**:

```
  60 seeds:  naive +$27    exact $-135
 120 seeds:  naive +$102   exact $-206
```

They differ because every trade is cut by a different factor, so a heavily-cut trade
carrying a large +r dominates the exact sum while the aggregate throws that away.
An earlier version of this diagnostic inferred "saved money" from the subset's
negative weighted R while the exact sum said otherwise; corrected before publishing.

Three further reasons the dollar sign here means very little:

1. **Sample size.** 36 and 79 cut trades — BELOW this ledger's own ~100-trade
   threshold at both sizes. The sign is unmeasured, not negative.
2. **The system is negative-expectancy overall**, so any dollar delta is
   noise-dominated. This harness measures internal consistency, never edge.
3. **maxDD rising is a path artefact, not more risk.** Shrinking some trades lowers
   the equity peak, so later troughs sit deeper below it even though every
   individual position got smaller. Per-trade risk strictly fell.

What IS consistent across both sizes: **per-trade variance falls** ($248.78→$234.23
and $200.88→$183.19), which is what a location-based risk reduction exists to do.
M52 ships as a **fidelity** fix on the same footing as M58 — the course states the
rule, and an R-multiple backtest on synthetic data structurally cannot validate a
sizing rule. Recorded as a negative dollar result rather than dropped (§10).

### Probe false failures, recorded so they are not repeated

Three failures in my own M52 probes, none of them a code defect (§6: audit a
failing probe before it becomes a finding):

- `pos 0.55 → equilibrium` failed: the fixture computes `90 + 20*0.55` and
  `20*0.55` is `11.000000000000002` in binary floating point, so the true position
  is `0.5500000000000004` → correctly 'premium'. Boundaries are now probed from
  just inside and just outside. An exact-boundary assertion on a float-derived
  quantity tests the representation, not the band.
- A label/sizing agreement check reported 51 disagreements because the floor
  condition was written `p <= 0.5`. For a LONG the floor spans the whole
  equilibrium band *and* the wrong half above it — the floor is "not deep enough
  into discount", not "below the midpoint".
- A strict-ordering check failed 1156/1235: `no-trade` carries `gradeMult 0`, so

- Separately, `position_pct` is reported rounded to 2dp while the zone label is

TWO REAL BUGS FOUND WHILE WIRING (both the same hazard, both in code I had just
written, both caught by checking the hazard rather than trusting the guard)

  null. With invalidation_side 'above', a level of 0 makes `close > 0` true on
  EVERY call — the bias is invalidated constantly while hysteresis looks like it
  is working. It appeared in db.js:setBiasState and, identically, in

  disagreement of 0 must not be read as "no disagreement".

    v === null || v === undefined || v === '' || !Number.isFinite(Number(v)) ? null : Number(v)
  The unit probe covered NaN but not null. Lesson recorded: test null AND NaN AND
  undefined AND '' for every numeric coercion.

The app was already Vercel-shaped: api/index.js exports the same Express handler

THE ONE HAZARD THAT MATTERS
  With VERCEL set and no remote URL, src/db.js falls back to file:/tmp/journal.db.
  The platform wipes /tmp on every cold start and does not share it between
  instances, so a deployment boots, returns 200, creates a workspace, accepts
  trades — and forgets all of them. Nothing errors. For a journal that is the worst
  available failure mode, so it is now reported rather than left to a boot log:
    · the boot log prints a !! EPHEMERAL DATABASE !! block
    · GET /api/health returns database.persistent=false plus a warning string
  It is deliberately NOT made fatal: a demo deploy should still come up.

ADDED

    deploy is check it came up, and the two silently-wrong things on a serverless
    host are exactly what it reports: database.persistent (ephemeral /tmp) and
    demo_data (TJ_OFFLINE_CANDLES shipping synthetic bars to production). DB URL
    is redacted of credentials before it is returned.
  .vercelignore — keeps scripts/, docs/, data/ and analysis/ out of the upload.
  DEPLOY.md — required env vars and their precedence, both deploy paths, the
    post-deploy verification order, the cron, and the known limitations stated up
    front rather than discovered later.
  scripts/vercel-check.js extended 9 -> 18 assertions, covering all three database
    configurations and the demo flag. The non-default ones run in CHILD PROCESSES

 M5 — KILLZONE DST FIX: SHIPPED AS CORRECT, AND IT MEASURES *WORSE*
 This is the single most informative result in the audit. Read it before quoting
 any other number in this file.
================================================================================
Applied analysis/0002-fix-killzone-dst.patch. The fix is unambiguously correct
against the course: the three windows were hardcoded UTC hours pinned to three
DIFFERENT offsets (London to EDT, NY AM to EST), so they could never all be right
at once and drifted an hour every time the US changed clocks. They are now stated
in New York time and converted at call time via Intl + America/New_York, so the ET
window is fixed and the UTC label moves:

    EST (winter)  London 07:00-10:00 UTC   NY AM 12:00-15:00 UTC   NY PM 15:00-17:00 UTC
    EDT (summer)  London 06:00-09:00 UTC   NY AM 11:00-14:00 UTC   NY PM 14:00-16:00 UTC

Measured by running the SAME harness twice, reversing the patch in between, so the
delta is attributable to M5 alone (--seeds 60 --bars 1500 --window 300):

    | metric                  | WITHOUT M5   | WITH M5      |
    | n                       | 122          | 106          |
    | win rate                | 18.9 %       | 16.0 %       |
    | expectancy              | -0.0011R     | -0.0935R     |
    | profit factor           | 1.00         | 0.87         |
    | grade A+ n / exp        | 15 / +0.1355R| 15 / -0.2603R|
    | flat 1% dollars         | $-14         | $-992        |
    | grade-sized dollars     | $-83         | $-1110       |
    | trades hitting target   | 11           | 8            |
    | trades hitting stop     | 83           | 78           |

A correct fix to the course's timing discipline made every headline number worse.

WHY, AND WHY IT IS NOT EVIDENCE AGAINST THE FIX. synth.js injects regime switching,
volatility clustering, liquidity sweeps and displacement legs — but it does NOT
inject session-of-day behaviour. There is no London open in the data, no New York
lunch lull, no Friday liquidity withdrawal. So shifting the eligible window by one
hour cannot find real session edge; it can only select a DIFFERENT SUBSET of bars
from a series that has no session structure. n fell 122 -> 106 and the subset that
remained happened to be worse. That is sampling noise on data that cannot reward
the fix, not a refutation of it.

THE GENERAL RULE THIS ESTABLISHES, and it applies to every remaining fidelity fix:
**the synthetic harness cannot validate course-fidelity changes.** It can only
detect changes that alter internal consistency. A fix that makes the bot more
faithful to the course will register as a random walk in these numbers — sometimes
up, sometimes down — because the thing being fixed (real session behaviour) is
absent from the data by construction.

So M5 ships on FIDELITY grounds, exactly as M9 and M52 did, with the negative
measurement recorded here rather than dropped. Reverting it to keep PF at 1.00
would be tuning the bot to a random-number generator.

 M125 — WHY rr_final IS DEGENERATE, MEASURED. New probe: probe-rr-degeneracy.js
 Nothing was fixed here. This is diagnosis, and it disproved the hypothesis I
 started with, which is the reason it is written down in full.
================================================================================
The harness's own line was the lead: `avg declared 28.25R · avg realised -0.094R ·
ratio -0.003`, with 8 trades hitting target against 78 hitting the stop. That is
the bot announcing a 28:1 reward and collecting nothing. HANDOFF-PROMPT §11 had
recorded it as a loose end and it never became a ledger row, so every expectancy
figure in this file has been denominated in an R unit the engine does not honour.
It is now M125, severity S1.

rr_final distribution over 41740 candidates (seeds 20, bars 1500, window 300):
    p10  1.58R    p25  3.75R    p50 15.79R    p75 50.68R
    p90 75.30R    p95 89.50R    p99 179.91R   max 286.40R   mean 31.17R
    56.9 % above 10R · 25.2 % above 50R

★ THE HYPOTHESIS I STARTED WITH WAS WRONG, AND THE PROBE SAYS SO.
rr = reward / risk, so I expected a tiny denominator: a sniper entry sitting almost
on the stop base. Measured, risk is p50 0.619 ATR, p25 0.417, and only 0.7 % of
candidates (120 of 16696) have a stop tighter than 0.25 ATR. **The stop is sound.**
Capping the stop width would have fixed nothing and damaged a correct structural
stop. This is why the distribution was measured before any threshold was chosen.

THE ACTUAL CAUSE — a stale swing level, not an aggressive target.
`setup.js:222` targets `sw.weak.price` as kind `weak_structure`, taken by
`strongWeak()` from `structure.last_swing_high` / `last_swing_low`, under the comment
"The MOST RECENT swing high and low are the levels that matter." They are not
recent. Over 8290 weak_structure targets:

    age of that swing, in bars (window = 300):
        p25 = 32      p50 = 60      p90 = 132     max = 217
    distance from entry, in ATR:
        p25 = 15.00   p50 = 32.12   p90 = 65.09   max = 118.9
    74.9 % lie beyond the measured reachability bound

So `last_swing_*` returns the last swing the DETECTOR CONFIRMED, which with
`swingMode:'bos'` and `nested:true` can be a historical extreme two hundred bars
old. That is not the opposing liquidity of the range price is dealing in, which is
what "target weak structure" means. M21's unshift at `setup.js:427-428` then forces
it to the front, and `runner = targets.find(t => t.rr >= minRR)` at `:432` selects
it — so the stale extreme wins the single-target choice M51 ships by default.
The five worst candidates all decompose the same way: rr = 286.40R with a perfectly
reasonable risk of 0.412 ATR and a reward of 118.94 ATR.

★ SECOND FINDING, WHICH CONSTRAINS THE FIX: a numeric cap CANNOT be calibrated here.
Measured 20-bar net travel on this generator is p50 7.646 ATR, p95 15.695 ATR
(26920 samples) — far beyond real 15m FX, because synth.js injects regime switching
and displacement legs by design. Deriving an ATR cap from that number would be
tuning the engine to a fantasy volatility regime: the same category of error as
reverting M5 to keep PF at 1.00. So the fix has to be STRUCTURAL — bound
`weak_structure` to the current dealing range, a level the code already computes at
`setup.js:408-416` as kind `range` — rather than a constant fitted to this data.
Recorded so that nobody "simplifies" M125 into a cap later.

NOT FIXED YET. Diagnosis, ledger row and probe are committed; the change itself is
next, and it must be measured against this baseline so the win-rate and expectancy
movement is attributable to it alone.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 M125 — THE FIX WAS IMPLEMENTED, MEASURED, AND REVERTED. Negative result.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The structural bound was implemented in setup.js targetPools(): classify a
weak_structure pool as `weak_structure_stale` when its level lies outside the
current dealing range, so M21's unshift (which matches kind === 'weak_structure'
exactly) no longer promotes it and the nearer pools plus the caller's `range`
target lead instead. M21's ordering was left byte-identical for the in-range case,
`strength` was deliberately not touched (changing it would perturb other scorers —
an unmeasured side effect), and with no dealing range available the old behaviour
was kept rather than guessed at.

  RESULT: it fired 0 times in 6176 weak-structure pools (0.0 %).
  rr_final p50 15.79R -> 14.03R, max 286.40R UNCHANGED, share above 10R
  56.9 % -> 56.4 %. That is noise, not a fix.

  why, measured:
    entry -> weak extreme        p25 14.06   p50 30.33   p90 66.04  ATR
    entry -> RANGE extreme       p25 24.78   p50 39.72   p90 69.05  ATR
    dealing-range SPAN itself    p25 12.1    p50 42.8    p90 63.0   ATR

The range extreme is FARTHER than the weak extreme, so the weak extreme is always
inside the range and a range bound can never trigger.

REVERTED, NOT SHIPPED. Code that never executes, sitting under a comment claiming
it fixes the declared-28R problem, is worse than no code: it reads as a fix to the
next person. After reverting, probe-rr-degeneracy.js reproduces the pre-fix baseline
exactly (p50 15.79R, max 286.40R, 56.9 % above 10R), which is the evidence the
revert was clean, and `git status extracted/` is empty against HEAD.

★ CORRECTED DIAGNOSIS — worse than the original, and it changes the priority order.
The degeneracy is NOT "the target lies outside the current range". It is that on this
generator a 300-bar window spans 40+ ATR (median range span 42.8 ATR), so EVERY
structural target inside it is tens of ATR away and no structural bound can bring
rr down. Two consequences, and they pull in different directions:

  (a) REAL ENGINE DEFECT, independent of data: the engine has no notion of a trade's
      own horizon. It computes rr = reward / risk and reports it with no question ever
      asked about whether price can reach the target while the position is open. It
      will announce 286R. That is a genuine defect on any data.

  (b) MEASUREMENT ARTIFACT: the observed MAGNITUDE (median 15.79R, 56.9 % above 10R)
      is dominated by synthetic range width. Real 15m FX over 300 bars (~3 days) does
      not span 42 ATR. So these figures cannot calibrate a fix, and they cannot even
      establish how severe the problem is on real data.

TWO DEAD ENDS NOW MEASURED AND RECORDED, so neither is retried:
  · a numeric rr cap — uncalibratable here, because measured 20-bar travel on the
    generator is p50 7.646 / p95 15.695 ATR, far beyond real 15m FX. Fitting a
    constant to that tunes the engine to the generator.
  · a dealing-range bound — inert, 0 of 6176, per the above.

The fix that remains needs a HORIZON concept: how long the trade is intended to be
held, and how far price can travel in that time, expressed in a unit that is stable
across instruments. Calibrating it requires real OHLCV.

★ THIS REORDERS THE AUDIT. Populating analysis/fixtures/ with real market data now
outranks every remaining episode-level fidelity fix, because until it exists:
  · M125 cannot be fixed, only described;
  · every fidelity fix ships on the argument from the course alone, with the harness
    able to register it as an improvement or a regression purely by chance (M5);
  · and no expectancy figure in this file means anything about real trading.
That is not a reason to stop fixing fidelity — the course is the specification and
M5 was correct to ship — but it is the reason no performance claim can be made.

  · MONDAY supports Ep 19 outright: the worst win rate in the sample (8.3 %) and the
    second-worst expectancy (-0.7195R). "Lower trading volume", "a lot of traps".
  · FRIDAY CONTRADICTS it: Friday is the BEST day in the sample — highest win rate
    (33.3 %), positive expectancy (+0.2649R), PF 1.64. Ep 19's reasoning ("lower

implementation of the course is still the right default — the course is the
specification, and M5 shipped on exactly that basis — but "finishing the todos will
make it profitable" is not supported, and in this one measured case the faithful
implementation would have made the sample worse.

 M124 FIXED — chart() and analyse() agreed to disagree, and the cause was the
 entry-series DEPTH, not td.conflicts. Last known failing assertion in the repo.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ROOT CAUSE. analyse() fetched a hardcoded 600 entry bars. chart() fetched
clamp(opts.bars || 400, 80, 1200). The same request with bars=400 therefore made the
chart analyse a 400-bar window and the analysis strip a 600-bar one. Different windows
-> different structure -> different topdown direction -> DIFFERENT ACTION ON THE SAME
BAR. Measured before the fix, EURUSD 15m at 2026-10-10T07:15Z:

    analyse   direction 1 · status confirmed · blocked false            -> BUY
    chart     direction 0 · status waiting   · blocked true             -> NO TRADE
              conflicts: no-trigger[BLOCKS], chase[BLOCKS]

The recorded diagnosis blamed `td.conflicts`. That was the SYMPTOM: the conflicts
follow from the direction, and the direction followed from the window. Fixing the
conflict logic would have treated the downstream effect.

The comment above chart()'s fetch already claimed "the same series lengths the analyse
endpoint uses, so the two payloads can never disagree about the same market (they did
once: the chart asked for 200 higher-timeframe candles...)". An earlier fix had made
that true for the BIAS and TRIGGER series and it was still false for the ENTRY series,
which is the one that decides direction. A claim like that has to be enforced by a
shared constant, not by two literals that happen to agree — so there is now one
METHOD_ENTRY_BARS = 600 read by both paths.

`opts.bars` IS A DISPLAY PARAMETER and is now kept strictly separate from how deep the
method reads. The chart still returns the candles asked for: sliced from the method
series when bars <= 600, fetched separately for display only when the caller wants more
drawn than the method reads, so the extra depth never feeds the analysis. Making the
chart ignore `bars` entirely would have been a regression dressed as a fix.

 M69 / M63 APPLIED — market shift is now a BREAK EVENT and a veto, not a trend
 label and a 12-point weight. The FIRST selectivity change out of seven that moved

THE CHANGE. Ep 17: "two things… liquidity sweep and market shift." Ep 20: "we need to have
some form of market shift. That's the prerequisite. That's the non-negotiable for each one of
these entry models." The gate tested only that the structure LABEL agreed with the trade
direction. A label summarises the whole window and can read 'bullish' with no break in the
required direction on this leg, so the non-negotiable could be satisfied without the event it
names. The sweep — the other of the same two things — was ALREADY a veto, while the shift was a
12-point weight, so losing it cost 12 of 155 and the setup still cleared the bar.

  setup.js: trendAligned now reads structure.last_break.dir against the direction the trade
  needs ('up' for a long, 'down' for a short) instead of the trend label. The break machinery
  was already correct — smc.js detects the break on the CLOSE, which is exactly his "a
  candlestick… where it closed above the last lower high". Only the gate read the label.
  A veto was added next to the sweep's so the pair is symmetric.

  IMPLEMENTED AS A VETO, WHICH IS STRONGER THAN WHAT THE LEDGER FIRST PRESCRIBED ("a hard
  condition for an A/A+ grade"). Documented judgment, three reasons: the course calls it the
  non-negotiable for EACH ENTRY MODEL, not a grade qualifier; this file's own stated rule is
  that non-negotiables are booleans in `vetoes` while the score only ranks candidates; and a
  grade cap would have left Ep 17's two things asymmetric, one vetoed and one weighted.

  M8's rule governs and was tested: MISSING DATA MUST NOT VETO. smc.analyse() returns a
  short-series sentinel with no `structure` key, and a gate firing on it would stand down every
  analysis of a short series. `structureKnown` separates "no shift observed" from "cannot know".
