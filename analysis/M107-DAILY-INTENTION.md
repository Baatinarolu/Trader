## Saved course basis and limits

Saved **Ep28** (S3): *“you can set **one clear rule for the day**… keep it as **one simple sentence**, and you want to make it **process-oriented**. You want to make it **behavioral**. You want to make it so that it's **within your control**. You can't say stupid stuff like *'Today, I'm going to make $5,000 from the market.'* No, **that's outside of your control**… that's an **outcome-focused goal**, which only happens as a **byproduct** of you following the process”*, with the four examples the episode itself gives: *“Today I follow my plan”*, *“Today I will take only A+ setups”*, *“Today I will journal every trade”*, *“Today I will not revenge trade.”* `grep -rni intention src/` returned **0 hits**; the nearest analogue `journal_entries.tomorrow` (`db.js:188`) is a next-day focus, not a same-day commitment. See `EPISODE-AUDIT.md`; no new fetch, invented timestamp or unavailable transcript.

The recorded words are the trader's **own writing**. A saved example is the only thing labelled process-oriented; free text is stored **exactly as written** and explicitly **unverified**, with the bounded classifier's own cues beside it. This is not an NLP judge, not verified discipline or plan adherence, not a score, percentage, streak or adherence tally, and **not a gate or permission of any kind**: it does not admit, refuse or size a trade and has no authority over risk, caps, routine, the core, grades, history or journal text. The app's own rules (one sentence, same UTC day, the money-target refusal) are **APP policy**, not quoted course laws. `journal_entries.tomorrow`, M64's morning check-in, M89 emotion capture, M105's identity/quarter and M106's fresh in-session gate stay separate mechanisms.

## Implemented mechanisms

1. **Owned immutable intention series.** Additive `daily_intentions`: append-only versioned rows with `human_declared` first-party records and `imported_unverified` archives. SQL UPDATE facts immutable (FK nullification the only exception); owner deletion cascades, account deletion SET NULL retains the row for the owner's own readonly history and never re-attaches it to a foreign account. Not sealed against database-admin tampering. Nothing is backfilled from notes, `journal_entries.tomorrow`, profit, adherence or legacy journal text, and no intention is defaulted or carried over between days.
2. **Same-day commitment contract.** `PUT /intention` writes **today only** with `{template}` or `{intention}` plus the displayed 64-hex `revision`; no caller clock, day, source, owner or authority is accepted. Yesterday and tomorrow are refused (`dayKey` refuses malformed and future days), an identical repeat is a no-op, a stale revision answers 409 with nothing written, and a store failure answers 503 without claiming a receipt.
3. **Curated templates are the only "process-oriented" label.** The four saved Ep28 examples (`follow_plan`, `a_plus_only`, `journal_every_trade`, `no_revenge`) are stored as `process_template`; the UI, the card and the export all say a saved example is what the app knows.
4. **Free text kept as written, explicitly unverified.** Anything else is stored verbatim with framing `unverified_free_text` or `outcome_language` and the classifier's cues (`sentences`, `money_target_sentences`, `outcome_language`, `prediction_language`, `control_language`, `chars`) plus named warnings (`outcome_language`, `prediction_language`, `multiple_sentences`, `long_text`, `not_process_verified`). The app never rewrites the words and never labels them process-oriented.
5. **Only the episode's own anti-example is refused.** An amount and an outcome **asserted in the same non-negated sentence** (`make $5,000 today`, `make 5%`, `win $1,000`) answers `intention_outcome_target` 400 with **nothing stored**, and names the process alternative. Everything else records: `have a green day` is stored and flagged as reading like an outcome, and `I will not turn a $500 loss into a revenge trade` is stored with no refusal because the amount sits inside a negation — the classifier refuses only what it can explain, and does not run sentiment analysis.
6. **Honest states and no invented days.** `unrecorded` (with `same_day` false on a day that passed), `recorded`, `unavailable` (a stored row that cannot be read back, e.g. a corrupt/future receipt) — never "failed", never a borrowed word. `last_recorded` is exposed as history only and is explicitly **not** today's intention. Reads embedded in `GET /briefing` and `GET /journal/:date` carry the same object and answer `unavailable` for a day that cannot be read (a day that has not happened), instead of failing the host surface — the inherited journal-day behaviour the M85 cohorts assert.
7. **Actual daily surfaces.** One shared card/editor/readonly-history on the Dashboard, the owned Journal day and Settings: the saved rule and the episode's own anti-example, current state and receipt, day (UTC), framing, warnings, retained record detail, an explicit `authority:'none'`, a today-only editor (saved example vs your own words, no preselected default), a reload that discards an unsaved pick, and a 409 path that keeps the draft. Past days are readonly and say why. Escaped text, no chart/provider fetch, no score/percentage/gate rendering.
8. **Portable history, finance unaffected.** Full JSON backup carries `daily_intentions`; restore labels every copy `imported_unverified` with the original day, clock, version and id retained as history, so restored words never become the receiving account's recorded day and never displace it. Invalid optional history warns while the financial import stays legal; a backup with no intention field is no warning at all; a `{records:[],unavailable:true}` marker warns instead of inventing days. M28's `tomorrow` field, trade CSV and the unchanged 20MB/25MB budgets are untouched.

## Initial errors kept, not erased by closure

- **The one real regression, found by an unchanged cohort and fixed in the app.** The first full M107 re-run measured the inherited M85 cohorts at **32/1** and **10/2** where M106 had 33/0 and 22/3. Root cause: `viewOn` refused a requested day that has not happened **by throwing**, so `GET /journal/:date` — which had always answered an empty 0/0 day — began failing its own day read. Fix: the journal-day and briefing surfaces embed the intention through `Intention.optional(...,{date})`, which answers an explicit `unavailable` payload naming the refusal, while the intention's **own** route keeps the strict 400. Both cohorts then reproduced M106 exactly (596/156, 824/4, 0 per-script diffs), and a new consumers assertion (`the journal day still answers a day that has not happened…`) locks the behaviour. The failure, the fix and the control are all retained here rather than re-pointed.
- The first M107 migration probe hung its first child to the 30s timeout: the fixture wrote the parent's state gate with **POST** where the M106 route is **PUT**, so express answered an HTML 404, `JSON.parse` threw, and the never-closed server kept the child alive. Fixed fixture-side (PUT for the state-gate and intention writes, plus a force-exit on the error path); the app was not touched for it.
- Migration-probe fixture traps fixed probe-side: the unique constraint creates its own `sqlite_autoindex_daily_intentions_1` (4 new objects, not 3), the copy-pasted trade count was one too high, and the state-gate view's `age_minutes` is a live clock read (compared modulo that derived field, like the M106 analogue).
- UI-probe traps fixed probe-side: jsdom does not run a radio's default action for a synthetic click (the check must be set and `change` dispatched, which jsdom fires nothing for), the editor re-creates its nodes on redraw so stale references silently send the previous pick, the settings helper is called as `Views.intention?.feedback` (the source assertion had to match the optional chain), and the retained-record detail assertion counted across modals.
- The orphan expectation was corrected to the module's actual, honest behaviour: a detached original row keeps its **true words, framing and receipt** and is disclosed with `account_id: null` in the owner's own readonly history (it is not "unreadable"), and the probe now additionally asserts it can never be claimed by a deleted desk (404) or leak into another owner's scope.
- Third-party OHLCV fixtures are gitignored and re-fetched per session; `npm ci` reproduces dependencies.
